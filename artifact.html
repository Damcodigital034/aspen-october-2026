Most carrier scorecards track cycle time, cost-per-file, and volume handled. Fewer track the metric that most closely correlates with downstream file quality. Revision request rate. How often a partner's submitted files come back for correction before the carrier desk can process them. A high revision rate signals that the partner's QA isn't catching what the carrier desk will catch. The gap between what gets submitted and what gets accepted is where desk rework hours accumulate - and where the real operational cost of the relationship hides. Partners with strong pre-delivery review usually have low revision rates. Partners without it usually don't. If your scorecard doesn't include revision request rate as a standing column, that's the piece worth adding before next year's evaluation cycle. #ClaimsWithAspen #ClaimsIntelligence #VendorPerformance

“The cheapest partner in the market is cheap because something in the operation isn't there. Usually QA. Sometimes training. Sometimes communication discipline. Sometimes all three.

I get asked why Aspen isn't the lowest-priced option in the IA market. That's the answer — and here's the rest of it.

The cost of those gaps doesn't disappear. It moves. It shows up later as supplements, reopens, complaint files, regulatory inquiries, and renewal losses that never trace cleanly back to the original decision.

We built the operation to produce files that close once and stay closed. That costs a little more on the front end. It costs less in total.

The carriers who run that math over a full year almost always land in the same place: the cheapest option isn't actually cheap. It just charges for the work it isn't doing in a different line of your P&L.

So when you price a claims partner, are you pricing the file — or the full cost of that file a year later?”
